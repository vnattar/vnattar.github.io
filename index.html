FoodVibes: Supply Chain Traceability & Geospatial Analytics

Traceability and accountability are key features necessary for food supply chain optimization. The global nature of these supply chains, which involve multiple stakeholders and low profit margin, make it hard to reliably track products and relevant information. I led a small team of interns and researchers on project FoodVibes to explore how we can leverage wireless sensing, communication, IoT, satellites, and geospatial analytics to enable sustainable, secure and transparent food supply chains.

Quick Links:

  • Open Source Repos:FoodVibes Git Repo | TerraTrace Git Repo | FarmVibes Project | SatExt Git Repo
  • Publications: Re-Inventing the Food Supply Chain with IoT | Fresh Sense: Wireless pallet-level produce sensing | TerraTrace: Land Use Monitoring | Hybridized anti-forgery DNA tags
  • Research Interns: Upinder Kaur (Purdue), Angela Busheska (Lafayette College), Ali Saffari (UW), Nakul Garg (UMD), and Nazish Naeem (MIT)

Food Supply Chain Traceability

  • FoodVibes-ai: An open-source tool that offers food and agricultural businesses an end-to-end supply chain traceability solution. The tool builds on trusted ledgers and geospatial analytics to track the When, What and Where of a product through its lifetime and bridge the critical information gaps in the supply chains.
  • Regulatory Compliance: Adopts strategies like the "one step forward and one step backward" association prescribed by the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA).
  • EUDR Support: Represented Microsoft Research in UN-affiliated DIASCA working groups shaping global policy for tech-enabled regulatory compliance in Agri-Food.
  • DIASCA White paper contributions: Forest Monitoring guidelines | Farmer Income and Production Cost Tracking

Wireless Sensing and IoT for Agri-Food Industry:

  • Fresh Sense presents a novel way of using wireless signals for pallet level produce lifetime monitoring during storage and transit of produce.
  • Worked with Nelson Farms and Loftus Farms to implement and validate IoT and traceability solutions to solve real world bulk-produce traceability challenges.
  • Our team's work on Hybridized DNA tags for raw-material tracing was published in Nature Communications

Satellite Imagery and Geospatial Analytics:

In addition to traceability, I also got to work with a team of interns and researchers on core research problems around enabling remote and satellite-based sensing for the Agri-Food industry. The projects were primarily around satellite-based land use monitoring; deforestation monitoring , these solve hard problems like differentiating forests, plantations and farms and other vegetation from satellite imagery; and SatExt; spextral extension and superresplution for extenting satellite imagery to high temporal, spatial and spectral coverage.

Project Eclipse: Hyperlocal Air Quality Sensing

Poor air quality is linked to various health issues, but few cities have the tools to measure air quality in real-time and at the neighborhood scale. Through this project I helped design, deploy and innovate solutions for hyperlocal, city-wide air quality monitoring.

Quick Links: Project Eclipse | Publication on device and analytics | Dataset on Planetary Computer

Project Goals:

  • Co-developed and deployed a hyperlocal air-quality sensing network across Chicago.
  • Developed infrastructure-independent fully-wireless sensors that harvested solar energy, communicated over LTE, and could be easily deployed.
  • Partnered with JCDecaux and local community groups to instrument 100+ transit shelters with air-quality sensors.
  • Real-time data analytics and calibration to create knowledge-driven understanding of local air quality conditions.
  • Enable citizen science to understand pollution's impact on local communities

As a part of this project, I got to innovate with an interdisciplinary team of researchers, interns, local environmental agencies and community groups

RF Bandaid: Fully-Analog Battery-Free Sensing and Communication

As a part of the Medical Devices Group at MSR in 2016, I developed a fully-analog, wearable and passive RF sensor for continuous physiological monitoring and open sourced the design. The vision for this work is to develop a bandaid-like device that can be worn on the body for bed-side monitoring of heart rate, breathing rate, temperature and audio. I continue to work in this area of analog, battery-free sensing and communication with my Rockstar Academic Collaborators across the world - thanks for keepign me excited folks!

Quick Links: RF Bandaid Paper | Localization Paper | Beat Frequency Paper | More in Google Scholar

Highlights:

The RFBandaid is first of its-kind in its design and capabilities. It is a fully-analog, battery-free, sensor platform. The key innovations of the RFBandaid include:
  • Ultra-Low Power Energy Harvesting: Device operates at 35 to 160 micro Watts power budget, harvested from 915MHz RF signals
  • Open Sourced Plug-and-Play Analog Design: Flexible sensor input analog platform that interfaces with different types of sensors (like heart rate, breathing rate, temperature, audio).
  • Fully-Analog Backscatter communication: Encodes sensor data in the frequency domain, eliminating the need for power-hungry ADCs and microcontrollers
  • Distinguished Recognition: Received distinguished paper award at UbiComp 2019

Research Extensions:

  • Ultra-low Latency Localization: With Jingxian Wang in 2020, demonstrated sub-centimeter location tracking at sub-millisecond latency
  • Beat Frequency Identification: Collaboration with Uppsala University in 2021 created a novel identification method for analog tags

FarmAble: Innovation to Support Local Farm-to-Table Ecosystem

As a part of the University of Washington - Global Innovation Exchange program, I led two innovative student teams focused on bridging the gap between local farmers and restaurants through technology. These Microsoft-sponsored projects addressed critical challenges in the farm-to-table ecosystem.

Quick Links: UW GIX Project Page | Project Poster | Frontend Repo | Backend Repo

Problem:

In today's farm-to-table scene, restaurants face challenges sourcing local produce and farmers struggle to market their sustainable produce effectively. This gap leads to waste and inefficiency across the supply chain.

Approach:

The team conducted extensive research and interviews across food supply chains to create prototypes that would provide value to three key stakeholders: farmers, restaurateurs, and diners.

Solution:

  • Timestamped Produce Tracking: Real-time tracking of produce from farm to restaurant to ensure freshness and transparency
  • QR Code Scanning: Enables restaurants to view detailed produce information and place orders seamlessly
  • Sustainability Dashboard: Diners can view sustainability information and understand the environmental impact of their food choices
  • Feedback Loop: Direct communication channel allowing diners to leave feedback to both farmers and restaurants
  • AgTech Mobile Platform: Mobile-first design optimized for use in restaurant and farm environments

Key Achievements:

  • Two Successful Cohorts: Led student teams in 2023 and 2024 to develop comprehensive solutions
  • Full-Stack Development: Complete mobile application with frontend and backend infrastructure
  • User-Centered Design and Testing: Research-driven approach based on stakeholder interviews and market analysis

Wireless Power Transfer, Low-Power RF Communication & Brain-Computer-Interface

Sept 2013 - Dec 2018

Research Focus: Brain-Computer-Spinal Interface (BCSI)

My PhD research focussed on a closed loop neural interface for rehabilitation and reanimation of paralyzed limbs in patients with spinal cord injury. The BCSI system development provides a system level block diagram and functional illustration along with the devices that I developed. The system is designed to stimulate in the spinal cord based on intentions decoded from neural signals recorded in the brain and close the break in communication created by a spinal cord injury.

Quick Links: BCI Publications:Dissertation Document | Brain-Computer-Spinal-Interface | NeuralCLIP Paper | More Wireless Power Transfer and Communications Papers: Google Scholar

Key Innovations:

  • Wireless Power Transfer: Integrated wireless power transfer at HF (13.56MHz) for power delivery to implants
  • Ultra-low Power Communication: Optimized UHF (915MHz) Backscatter communication for low-power implants
  • Closed-loop Neural Recording and Stimulation: Real-time FPGA algorithms built on approximate computing for neural signal processing
  • NeuralCLIP Prototype: Demonstrates stimulation triggered by on-device decoding of action intent from Local Field Potentials (LFP) recorded in the motor cortex

Technical Achievements:

To accommodate for variation in power available from a wireless power transfer link, the closed-loop system is built on novel approximate computation blocks on a FPGA. I was awarded a graduate innovation fellowship from the Washington Research Foundation to support this work on the implantable neural interface device.

Award: Washington Research Foundation Fellowship 2017: Innovation Graduate Fellowship in Neuroengineering

NeuralCLIP Prototype

The modular neural interface device NeuralCLIP which was developed as a part of this project was awarded the Madrona prize runner up in 2018.

Award: Madrona Prize 2018 - Implantable Wireless Brain-Computer Interface

Apart from this core research, I worked on other projects including energy harvesting from RF and NFC for wearable sensors, analog backscatter, wireless power transfer using phased array systems and low-overhead echolocation for localizing receivers in a HF phased array power transfer system. I also developed an FPGA implementation of the RFID EPC Gen2 protocol for performing block read and write with an Impinj R1000 RFID reader.
